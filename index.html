7.1 Introduction: why do things fall, and how do planets move?

Everything thrown up comes back down. Galileo (1564–1642) showed that all bodies, whatever their mass, fall towards the Earth with the same constant acceleration. He rolled bodies down inclined planes to find this value, close to the modern value of g.

A seemingly unrelated mystery: the stars keep fixed positions year after year, but a few bright objects, the planets, wander against the background of stars. How do they move?

7.2 Kepler’s Law 1: Law of Orbits

Statement: All planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun situated at one of the foci of the ellipse.

An ellipse is drawn with a string of fixed length with its ends fixed at two points F₁ and F₂ (the foci) and a pencil keeping the string taut. So for any point T on it, F₁T + F₂T = constant = 2a. The midpoint O is the centre, PO = OA = a is the semi-major axis. P is the perihelion (closest to Sun) and A is the aphelion (farthest). For a circle, the two foci merge and a becomes the radius.

Try: drag the planet around the orbit, then change the shape with the slider.

Notice: however you move the planet, d₁ + d₂ stays equal to 2a. Set e = 0 and the orbit becomes a circle.

7.2 Kepler’s Law 2: Law of Areas

Statement: The line that joins any planet to the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal intervals of time.

So a planet moves slower when farther from the Sun and faster when nearer. The sim cuts one orbit into 12 equal time intervals. Each coloured wedge is swept in the same time, so every wedge has the same area.

Why? Conservation of angular momentum

In time Δt the area swept is ΔA = ½ (r × vΔt). So ΔA/Δt = ½ (r × p)/m = L/2m. Gravity is a central force (along r), so torque is zero and L is constant. Hence ΔA/Δt is constant.

At perihelion and aphelion r and v are perpendicular, so m·vₚ·rₚ = m·vₐ·rₐ, i.e. vₚ/vₐ = rₐ/rₚ. Since rₐ > rₚ, we get vₚ > vₐ.

7.2 Kepler’s Law 3: Law of Periods

Statement: The square of the time period of revolution of a planet is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit.

T² ∝ a³ or T²/a³ = constant

Build your own planet. T is in years and a in AU (Earth’s distance, 1 AU ≈ 1.5 × 10¹¹ m). The sun-centred picture shows Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars and your planet.

Double a and T becomes 2√2 ≈ 2.83 times larger, not 2 times. Far planets are slow.

Newton’s connection (7.3)

Newton reasoned that the Moon, in orbit of radius Rₘ, has a centripetal acceleration aₘ = V²/Rₘ = 4π²Rₘ/T². With T ≈ 27.3 days and Rₘ ≈ 3.84 × 10⁸ m, aₘ is much smaller than g at Earth’s surface. This led to the Universal Law of Gravitation.
